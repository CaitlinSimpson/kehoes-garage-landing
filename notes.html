Kehoe's Garage — Behind the Build

Case G: Kehoe's Garage

Task 1: Think it through before you build

Hunch (before AI)

Who would pay for a generic version of this solution, and why?

Whilst this solution would benefit all garages, I believe it would benefit smaller independent garages with fewer staff (both mechanics and office). The owner of the garage would pay for the application as it directly bemnefits business through providing better customer service and is more time effective. This allows the mechanics on the ground to update the progress of their own work, admin staff to use that data to interact with customers, and customers to track the progress of ther work. Franchises and bigger garages also benefit from this as it would allow them to streamline processes and use the ame software across branches.

Niche, buyer and users

Niche: Independent garages in Ireland with approximately 10–15 staff, rather than large franchise groups.

Buyer: The garage owner, who would pay for a system intended to reduce interruptions, improve the flow of repairs and provide better customer service.

Daily users: Mechanics update repair stages; office staff monitor outstanding work and respond to customers; customers check the progress of their own vehicles.

Invented customer pain statement: “When reception is busy or someone is on lunch, we end up pulling mechanics off repairs to answer calls. Meanwhile, cars waiting for parts can sit for days without an update.”

Technology and current process

The proposed product is a mobile-friendly job-tracking application with three views: a workshop board, an office dashboard, and a limited customer status portal.

The case describes frequent calls about repair progress, interruptions to mechanics, and cars being overlooked while waiting for parts. The exact current process is not fully specified in the case, so verbal updates, phone calls and existing job records are assumptions to validate.

Two existing products

Escargo: Escargo website. Garage software covering vehicle jobs, parts requests and customer repair-status updates.

LaunchControl: LaunchControl website. Workshop-management software with digital job sheets, technician tools, scheduling and customer communication.

Date checked: Confirm the date you actually checked each website.

Proposed business model — assumption

Proposed pricing: €99 one-off setup fee plus €49 per month per garage. The setup fee could cover configuration and onboarding; the monthly fee provides recurring revenue. These prices need to be validated with garage owners.

Lean PRD — five lines

  1. Hypothesis: Independent garages will show interest in a simple job tracker that reduces status-enquiry interruptions and flags cars delayed by parts.
  2. Audience: Independent Irish garages with 10–15 staff, several mechanics and a small office team.
  3. Call to action: “Join the waiting list.”
  4. Success measure: Number of relevant garage owners who join the waiting list after visiting the landing page.
  5. Out of scope: Online payments, invoicing, parts ordering, automatic messaging integrations and multi-branch management.

Alpha test

What I tried

I tested the Workshop, Office and Customer views using the nine fictional sample records. I moved cars through the repair stages and refreshed the page to confirm that changes were saved. I checked the overdue-parts warning, the ready-to-collect count, the customer lookup and the layout on my phone.

One thing that confused me

The “Days” button opens a text prompt, which requires typing. This is less convenient for mechanics using a workshop tablet with greasy hands.

What I changed

I did not make any changes after testing because the main functions worked as expected. The test identified that the “Days” prompt could be improved in a future version by using larger, simpler controls that require less typing.
